# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository centralizes the project management guidance used by OctoAcme. The documents describe a practical lifecycle for turning an idea into a delivered outcome: a project is first validated and authorized during initiation, converted into an actionable backlog during planning, executed with disciplined tracking and review, managed through risk and stakeholder communication, released with deployment safeguards, and improved through retrospectives. Together, these materials give the team a repeatable way to align on priorities, manage dependencies, and deliver value with transparency.

## Project lifecycle overview

OctoAcme’s approach starts with initiation, where the team confirms the problem, defines stakeholders, establishes measurable outcomes, and decides whether to move forward. The planning phase turns that idea into a prioritized backlog, milestone plan, and Definition of Done. Execution follows with daily tracking, sprint rhythms, pull-request workflow, testing, and issue management to keep delivery moving. Once work is ready, the team follows clear release and deployment practices to reduce risk, verify quality, and communicate changes. After each major milestone or release, the team uses retrospectives and action tracking to capture improvements and make the process more effective over time.

## Roles and personas

The project docs define how OctoAcme expects teams to work across functions. Product and project leads set outcomes and priorities, developers implement and validate the work, QA checks quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide business context and approvals. Clear ownership and communication are emphasized throughout so everyone understands responsibilities, dependencies, and escalation paths.

## Quality and delivery expectations

OctoAcme emphasizes quality throughout the lifecycle. Work should be broken into testable increments, accompanied by clear acceptance criteria, reviewed through pull requests, and validated with unit, integration, and smoke testing as appropriate. The project process also requires CI validation, security scanning, and manual QA where needed before releases. This helps keep execution predictable while maintaining a strong standard for reliability and customer value.

## Additional guidance

These documents are intended to be living artifacts for the team: keep project charters and plans current, review risks regularly, and use retrospectives to convert learnings into operational improvements. The repository acts as a central source of truth so new teammates can quickly understand the project management practices used by OctoAcme, while detailed process guidance remains available in the individual documents.
